1
A–B: Airway & Breathing
⚡ Major bleeding? Skip to Section 2, return here after.
A — Airway
  1. Check & clear the airway — remove any visible obstruction.
  2. Choking? Abdominal thrusts from behind.
  3. Unresponsive? Open airway:
    • Jaw-Thrust or Head-Tilt, Chin-Lift
B — Breathing
  1. No breath + no pulse?
    • CPR: 30 compressions, 2 breaths — repeat
    • Ask 911 or bystanders for an AED
    • Maintain CPR → go to Section 2
  2. No breath, has pulse?
    • 1 rescue breath every 6 seconds
    • Maintain rescue breaths → go to Section 2
  3. Breathing + pulse present? → Go to Section 2
2
C — Bleeding / Circulation
  1. Head wound?
    • ABD pads on wound · gentle pressure
    ⚠ Do NOT pack — brain tissue is fragile
  2. Neck, core, back, pelvis?
    • Pack z-folded gauze deep · ABD pads on top · firm pressure
  3. Arm or leg?
    • ABD pads · firm pressure
    • If direct pressure isn't stopping the bleeding → tourniquet as high as possible on limb
    • Not on a knee or elbow joint
    • Tighten until bleeding stops
    • Write the time on their skin
  4. Bleeding stopped? No → repeat 1–3  ·  Yes → Section 3
↩ Came here for urgent bleeding? Return to Section 1 — Airway.
3
D–E–G: Disability, Environment & Glucose
D — Disability (Neuro Check)
  1. Ask: name · time · location · what happened
    • Wrong/no answer → prepare for evacuation
  2. Check hands & feet — movement, sensation, warmth?
    • Any absent → minimize movement (spine/brain injury risk)
E — Environment
  1. Cold, wet, or major bleeding?
    • Remove wet clothing
    • Insulate from the ground
    • Wrap in space blanket
G — Glucose
  1. Anxious, confused, or sweating?
    • Administer glucose between teeth and cheeks
⚠ Untreated hypoglycemia can cause cardiac arrest.
4
Evacuation
Evacuate immediately if any apply
Major bleeding or burns
Tourniquet or hemostatics used
Difficulty breathing
Confusion, agitation, anxiety
Hands/feet numb or immobile
Vitals outside normal ranges
Never be too proud to call for a helicopter and Search & Rescue.

7
Blisters
  1. Pop with a sterile safety pin
  2. Leave intact skin — remove only loose/dead skin
  3. Clean with warm soapy water (if available)
  4. Clean again with an alcohol wipe
  5. Apply triple antibiotic over the blister
  6. Cover with a band-aid
  7. Cut 2+ moleskin "donuts" — place around blister, not on it. Stack to create space between blister and force causing blister.
  8. Apply Leukotape or duct tape over moleskin to secure
  9. Repeat steps 3–8 every 12–24 hrs
💡 Blister Prevention: At first sign of a hot spot, alcohol wipe the area and apply Leukotape directly over it.
