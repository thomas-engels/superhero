Airway & Breathing
⚡ Major bleeding? Skip ahead to Section 2 first — return here after.
  1. Check & clear the airway — remove any visible obstruction.
  2. Choking? Abdominal thrusts from behind until clear.
  3. Unresponsive? Open the airway:
    Jaw-Thrust — or — Head-Tilt, Chin-Lift
    American Heart Association · BLS Provider Guidelines
    Head-Tilt, Chin-Lift (no suspected spine injury)
    1. 1Place one hand on the forehead and tilt the head back gently.
    2. 2Lift the chin upward with two fingers under the bony part — not soft tissue.
    Jaw-Thrust (suspected spine injury)
    1. 1Place fingers behind the angle of the jaw on both sides.
    2. 2Thrust the jaw forward without tilting the head or moving the neck.
    If jaw-thrust does not open the airway and there is no breathing, switch to head-tilt chin-lift — an open airway takes priority.

Airway & Breathing
✕ Critical
No breath + no pulse
  • CPR: 30 compressions + 2 breaths — repeat
  • Ask 911 or bystanders for an AED
  • Maintain CPR while moving to Section 2
American Heart Association · BLS Provider Guidelines (Adults)
  1. 1Heel of dominant hand on the lower half of the sternum. Second hand on top, fingers interlaced and lifted off the chest.
  2. 2Compress at least 2 inches (5 cm) deep at 100–120/min. Allow full chest recoil — don't lean between compressions.
  3. 3Minimize interruptions. Any pause (breaths, AED check) should be under 10 seconds.
  4. 4Rescue breaths: tilt head, lift chin, create seal over mouth, give 1 breath over 1 second — chest should visibly rise.
  5. 5Continue 30:2 until AED is ready, EMS takes over, patient regains normal breathing, or you are physically unable to continue.
AED: power on, attach pads, follow prompts. Resume compressions immediately after any shock — do not wait to check pulse.
⚠ Caution
No breath, has pulse
  • 1 rescue breath every 6 seconds
  • Monitor pulse continuously
  • Maintain rescue breaths while moving to Section 2
✓ Good
Breathing + pulse present

Bleeding / Circulation
  1. Head wound?
    ABD pads on wound · apply gentle pressure
  2. Neck, core, back, or pelvis?
    Pack hemostatic gauze deep into the wound · firm, sustained pressure
    Z-Medica · QuikClot® Combat Gauze IFU
    1. 1Identify the deepest point of the wound.
    2. 2Pack the gauze starting at the deepest point — use a finger to push it in firmly and continuously.
    3. 3Continue packing until the wound cavity is completely filled.
    4. 4Apply firm, direct pressure for a minimum of 3 minutes without releasing.
    5. 5Do not remove the gauze. Apply additional dressing over it and secure.
    ⚠ Hemostatic gauze is for junctional wounds (neck, groin, armpit, shoulder, pelvis) where a tourniquet cannot be applied.
  3. Arm or leg?
    ABD pads · firm pressure
    If still bleeding — apply tourniquet:
    🩺 Tourniquet Protocol
    1Apply above the bleeding — never on a joint (knee or elbow)
    2Tighten until bleeding completely stops
    3Write the time on their skin — 911 needs to know
    4Do NOT remove — only medical professionals can remove a tourniquet
    North American Rescue · CAT® Gen 7 Tourniquet IFU
    1. 1Slide the limb through the loop. Position 2–3 inches above the wound — never on a joint.
    2. 2Pull the free-running end of the self-adhering band as tight as possible, then press the velcro to secure it.
    3. 3Turn the windlass rod until bleeding completely stops or the distal pulse is no longer palpable.
    4. 4Lock the rod into the windlass clip. Secure the strap over the rod.
    5. 5Write the time of application on the windlass strap with a marker. Report time to EMS on arrival.
    ⚠ Do NOT remove in the field. Only EMS / medical personnel can safely remove a tourniquet — removal can cause sudden cardiac arrest from reperfusion.
  4. Still bleeding? Repeat steps 1–3. Controlled → move to Section 3.

🌡️
Disability · Env · Glucose
  1. Cold, wet, or major bleeding? Prevent hypothermia immediately:
    Remove wet clothing
    Insulate from the ground
    Wrap in space blanket — shiny side in
    Adventure Medical Kits · Heatsheets® Emergency Blanket IFU
    1. 1Remove wet clothing before wrapping when possible — wet fabric accelerates heat loss.
    2. 2Insulate from the ground first. Cold ground pulls heat faster than cold air. Use pack, clothing, or debris.
    3. 3Wrap the blanket fully around the body including the head, leaving only the face exposed. Tuck edges under.
    4. 4For severe hypothermia: a second blanket can form a sleeping bag-style wrap for full body coverage.
    The space blanket reflects up to 90% of radiated body heat. It is not an insulator on its own — always combine with ground insulation.

Disability · Env · Glucose
⚠ Check for hypoglycemia
Anxious, confused, or sweating?
  • Administer glucose between their teeth and cheeks
  • Allow it to absorb — do not give water yet
⚠ Untreated hypoglycemia can cause cardiac arrest. When in doubt, give glucose.

Evacuation
Evacuate immediately if any apply
Major bleeding or burns
Tourniquet or hemostatics used
Difficulty breathing
Confusion, agitation, anxiety
Hands/feet numb or immobile
Vitals outside normal ranges
Never be too proud to call for a helicopter and Search & Rescue.

Blisters
  1. Pop with a sterile safety pin
  2. Leave intact skin — remove only loose or dead skin
  3. Clean with warm soapy water, if available
  4. Clean again with an alcohol wipe
  5. Apply triple antibiotic over the blister
  6. Cover with a band-aid
  7. Cut 2+ moleskin "donuts" and place around the blister, not on it
    Stack donuts to create a raised ring that lifts pressure off the blister
  8. Apply Leukotape or duct tape over the moleskin to secure
  9. Repeat steps 3–8 every 12–24 hours
💡 Blister Prevention: At the first sign of a hot spot — alcohol wipe the area and apply Leukotape directly over it before a blister forms.
